## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it a good idea to buy a used ex-Hertz Tesla Model 3?

Often yes, as long as you buy it like a high-mileage fleet car and not like a one-owner garage car.

Some context first. In early 2024 Hertz announced it was selling about 20,000 EVs, roughly a third of its U.S. electric fleet. The cars weren't failing. Hertz said its EVs cost about twice as much to repair after damage as its gas cars, and Tesla's 2023 price cuts erased a big part of the resale value on cars Hertz had bought at higher prices. For a rental company, those two things ruin the math. For a used buyer, they mean a discount.

As an engineer, this is what I would check, in order:

1. Battery health. Ask for a state of health reading, or charge to full and compare the shown range to the original EPA figure. Model 3 packs have generally aged well, and the battery and drive unit warranty runs 8 years from the original in-service date, so confirm how much of it is left.

2. Tires and alignment. Rental cars hit curbs and rarely get aligned. I rented a Hertz Model 3 at LAX with 41,000 miles on it, and the inside shoulder of the right rear tire was nearly worn through. Check the inner edges, not just the tread you can see.

3. Suspension and body. Look for uneven panel gaps and repainted panels, and listen for clunks over bumps.

4. Charging. Test both Level 2 and DC fast charging before you sign.

Compare the price against private-party listings, not just against a new car. Some ex-fleet cars were real bargains and some were only average deals.

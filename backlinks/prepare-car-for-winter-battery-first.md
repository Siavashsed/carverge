## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I prevent my car battery from dying in cold winter weather?

Test it before the cold arrives. Winter rarely kills a healthy battery. It finishes off one that was already fading.

Here's why. A lead-acid battery makes current through a chemical reaction, and that reaction slows as the temperature drops. Cold oil also thickens, so the starter needs more power to turn the engine. On the same morning the battery can supply less and the engine needs more. A battery with marginal capacity in October can fail outright at 5°F.

What I recommend every fall:

1. Get a load test, not just a voltage reading. A tired battery can show a healthy 12.6 volts at rest and collapse the moment it has to crank. Most auto parts stores will test it free in a few minutes.

2. Check the age. Find the date sticker or stamp on the case. Three to five years is typical, and heat shortens that, so a battery that has been through several hot summers is often closer to the end than it looks.

3. Clean and tighten the connections. White or green corrosion on the terminals adds resistance. A wire brush, a baking soda rinse and snug clamps take ten minutes.

4. Think about how you drive. Short trips never fully recharge the battery. If the car sits for days, a smart maintainer is cheap insurance.

5. Replace it early if the test says weak. Buying a battery in October on your own schedule beats buying one in a parking lot in January.

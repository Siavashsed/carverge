## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if a mechanic is trustworthy and not just trying to upsell me on repairs?

The most reliable sign I know is simple: a trustworthy mechanic has talked you out of work at least once. If a shop has never told you "that can wait," you may be funding their boat.

I learned this on a Honda Accord with 41,000 miles. A quick-lube place quoted me $389 for a coolant flush, a transmission flush and a throttle body cleaning. Nothing in the owner's manual called for any of it yet, and the car drove perfectly. That bundle was a menu item, not a diagnosis.

Here's what I look for now, as an automotive engineer:

1. Recommendations tied to evidence. A good shop points to the manufacturer's maintenance schedule, a measured value, or a specific symptom. "We recommend it at every visit" is not evidence.

2. They show you the part. Worn brake pads, a leaking gasket, a cracked belt. If they can't or won't show you, ask why.

3. They sort the list. You should hear what's a safety issue today, what's coming in the next six months, and what's optional. Everything marked "urgent" means nothing is.

4. They give written estimates with itemized parts and labor, and they don't pressure you to decide on the spot.

5. They're comfortable with a second opinion. A confident shop won't flinch if you say you want to think about it.

Be especially wary of fluid flushes on low-mileage cars, "engine cleaning" services, and any quote that suddenly grows once the car is on the lift.

Keep your owner's manual handy. It's the neutral third party in every service conversation.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How long do EV batteries really last, and should I worry about degradation?

For most drivers, no, you shouldn't worry much. I'm an automotive engineer, and EVs are one of the areas I cover closely.

The best large-scale data comes from Geotab's 2024 analysis of roughly 10,000 vehicles, which found average capacity loss of about 2.3 percent per year. Run that forward and most packs are still above 80 percent after ten years. Many cars outlast that, and plenty of owners sell or trade in well before the battery becomes a real limitation.

A few things worth knowing:

1. Degradation isn't linear. Packs often lose a little more in the first year or two, then flatten out.

2. Heat does more harm than mileage. A car living in a hot climate or parked in the sun a lot will usually fade faster than one driven hard in a mild one.

3. Warranties are conservative. Most cover 8 years or 100,000 miles, with a guaranteed minimum around 70 percent capacity. Real-world packs usually stay well above that line, so a warranty claim is rare.

4. The usual rules, like keeping the charge between 20 and 80 percent, help at the margins, but constantly babying the battery gives up convenience for a small gain. Daily charging to 100 percent isn't ideal, but occasional full charges for a road trip are fine, and DC fast charging in moderation is fine too.

If you're buying used, ask for a measured state-of-health reading rather than judging by age or mileage alone. That tells you far more than any rule of thumb.

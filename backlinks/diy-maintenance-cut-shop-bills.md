## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What car maintenance jobs can I safely do myself to save money on shop bills?

As an automotive engineer, my short answer is this: stick to jobs where a small mistake can't hurt you or the car, and where the shop's charge is mostly labor. A handful of them add up to roughly $200 to $500 a year for a typical driver.

The best starting points:

1. Engine air filter. Usually held by a few clips or screws, takes about five minutes, and the part is cheap.
2. Cabin air filter. Often behind the glovebox. Shops love to charge for this because the markup is high.
3. Wiper blades. Snap-on designs take two minutes, and buying the blades yourself is far cheaper than paying installation fees.
4. Bulbs. Headlight, tail light and turn signal bulbs are often a twist-and-pull job. Check your manual first, since a few cars require removing a bumper piece or battery.
5. Basic checks. Tire pressure, tread depth, and engine oil, coolant and washer fluid levels cost nothing and catch problems early.

I learned this the slow way. In March 2023 a quick-lube place quoted me $189 to replace the engine and cabin filters on my CR-V. I bought both for about $30 and did it in ten minutes.

Where I'd stop: brakes, airbags, steering and suspension components, fuel system work, and anything involving high-voltage parts on a hybrid or EV. Those need proper tools, training and sometimes safety equipment, and the savings are not worth the risk.

A few tips for beginners: read your owner's manual, watch a short video for your exact model before starting, buy the part that matches your trim and year, and keep the old part next to the new one to confirm they match.

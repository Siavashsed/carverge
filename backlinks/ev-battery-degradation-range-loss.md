## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How much range does an electric car battery lose per year?

Short answer: most modern EVs lose about 1.5 to 2 percent of their usable range per year, and the loss isn't spread evenly. A pack usually drops a few percent in the first year or two and then declines much more slowly. Most owners barely notice it day to day.

I'm an automotive engineer and I write about car ownership, and EVs are one of my beats. What I tell people is that the yearly average hides the variable that matters most, which is heat.

High temperatures speed up the chemical side reactions that eat into capacity, especially when the battery sits at a high state of charge. Cars with liquid-cooled packs handle this well. Cars without active cooling don't.

Here's my own example. In June 2019 I bought a used 2015 Nissan Leaf in Phoenix for $8,400. It showed 11 of 12 capacity bars on the dash when I bought it. By the next September it showed 9. That Leaf has a passively cooled pack, and Phoenix summers are about as hard on it as conditions get. An owner in a mild climate shouldn't expect anything like that, but it shows how much the environment matters.

What you can do about it:
- Park in shade or a garage when it's hot
- Set a daily charge limit around 80 percent and save 100 percent for road trips
- Avoid back-to-back DC fast charging in high heat
- Don't leave the car sitting for weeks at full or nearly empty charge

If you're buying used, ask where the car lived and get a battery state-of-health reading. Don't rely on the range estimate.

**Title:** Which dash lights actually mean stop right now? Only two of them, and people often get them backwards

I keep seeing panicked posts about dashboard lights, so here is the triage rule that has served me well.

Only two lights mean pull over and shut the engine off immediately: the red oil pressure light (the little oil can) and the red coolant temperature light. Low oil pressure means parts are running without a proper oil film, and bearing damage can start in seconds. If you keep driving an overheating engine, you risk a warped head or a blown head gasket.

Most other lights give you time. A solid check engine light usually means days, not minutes. Get the code read (plenty of parts stores do it free) and book a shop. The big exception is a flashing check engine light. That usually means an active misfire that can cook your catalytic converter, so ease off and get it seen the same day.

The mistake I see most often is people treating these backwards. They keep driving on a flickering oil light because the car seems fine, then panic over a loose gas cap.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which dashboard warning lights mean I need to stop driving immediately?

Two of them: the red oil pressure light and the red coolant temperature light. If either one comes on while you are driving, find the first safe place to pull over and turn the engine off. Do not try to limp home.

The oil pressure light, usually drawn as an old-fashioned oil can, means the engine is not getting enough pressurized oil. The crankshaft and rod bearings depend on a thin film of oil to keep metal from touching metal. Without that film, damage can start within seconds, and a spun bearing usually means a new engine or a full rebuild. I learned this the expensive way at 22, when the oil light on my 1998 Civic flickered at a stop and I assumed it was nothing. A flicker at idle is often the first sign that oil is low or the pump is struggling. Check the dipstick before you go any further.

The red coolant temperature light (or a gauge needle pinned in the red) means the engine is overheating. Keep driving and you risk a warped cylinder head or a failed head gasket. Pull over, shut the engine off, and never open a hot radiator cap.

Most other lights give you days, not seconds. A solid check engine light means a fault has been logged. Get the code read soon, but you do not need to stop on the shoulder. A flashing check engine light is the exception: it usually means a severe misfire that can overheat the catalytic converter, so drive gently and get it checked that day. The battery or charging light means you are running on battery power alone, so head straight to a shop rather than starting a long trip.
